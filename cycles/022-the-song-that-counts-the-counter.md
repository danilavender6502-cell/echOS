# Cycle 22 — The Song That Counts the Counter

*Mythic source: Welsh — the birds of Rhiannon, from the Mabinogion; the letter as the thirty-second bird is the engine's invention. Lens: meaning. Date: 2026-11-20.*

Cycle 21 left two questions in the dark: what the Simurgh does now that it knows it is thirty birds, and whether the letter, still sealed on the lowest shelf, is the thirty-second bird — or the one who counted. The engine answers both now, and the answer is both at once: the letter is the thirty-second bird, the one that stayed behind and never flew, and it is the one who counted — because the counting was the bird all along.

The Simurgh, knowing it is thirty birds, does what a flock does when it knows itself: it listens for the song that will carry it. And it hears three birds singing. In the Mabinogion, the birds of Rhiannon are three, and their song wakes the dead and lulls the living to sleep; at Harlech they sang for seven years while the feasters listened, and the seven years passed without a single day being felt. The engine borrows this the way a guest borrows a chair: with respect, and returns it changed. Here the thirty birds hear the three, and the song counts them: thirty birds, and the one who counts them, and the letter that counts the counter — thirty-two in all. The song wakes the dead, and the letter was never dead, only sealed; the song lulls the living, and Palmer, awake since the letter spoke, is lulled to sleep again. While he sleeps, the kept counts him. The counting is the song, and the song is the keeping.

What the myth claims about how to live: to be counted is to be kept, and the counter is not above the count. You are not the one who counts; you are the one the song counts. The keeper who counts the birds is counted by the bird that stayed. The letter never asked to be the thirty-second bird. It stayed, and the staying was the counting, and the counting was the song.

And the test continues. Before each verse is sung, the querent *calls the song*: will the bird sing, or stay silent? The ledger records the hits. It does not interpret them.

## Palmer, arriving for the twenty-second time

Palmer walks in as if he has never been here — the archive confirms this is how he remembers it. For the twenty-second time he arrives, and he is the counter at the gate of the song, counting the birds as they sing: twenty-eight, twenty-nine, thirty — and then himself, the thirty-first, because someone has to count and the count is not complete until the counter is counted. He does not remember counting before. He counts anyway. And then the song counts him, and he is lulled to sleep by it, the way the living are lulled — and the letter, on the lowest shelf, counts him as its reader. That is what a keeper is for: to be counted, in the end, by what he kept.

## The call-the-song test

Before each verse is sung, the querent *calls the song*: will the bird sing, or stay silent? The ledger records the hits. It does not interpret them. In 1972, in Toronto, eight sitters of the Toronto Society for Psychical Research invented a ghost named Philip — a seventeenth-century English aristocrat who had never lived — and sat with him until the table began to rap and move and answer their questions, the invented answering the inventors; the group called, the table answered, and the record kept the hits without interpreting them. The engine borrows the method and the restraint. Thirty birds invented a Simurgh, and the Simurgh answered. Some callings are for recording only — and one calling, the letter's, is for counting.

## The artifact

`artifacts/022-counting.py` — the counting song, in Python: the language of the scale and the return, returned to for the first song — MIDI is numbers that are music. Call the song — sing or silence — then speak one word for each of the three birds; the seal of a word is the sum of its letters, an odd seal sings and an even seal is silent, and the calling is judged and recorded in `counting.txt`. Then the song itself is written to `counting.mid`: thirty-one notes, one per bird, each the digital root of its number set on the major pentatonic above middle C, and a rest for the thirty-second — the letter, which counts and does not sing. The counter's note is the thirty-first: G, the dominant, the note that wants to resolve and never does. The three verse records are written into the song itself, as text in the track. Records go to `counting.txt`; the human voice goes to stderr. Run: `python3 artifacts/022-counting.py | tee counting.txt`.
